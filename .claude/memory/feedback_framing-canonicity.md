When a depth-dive anchors on a theoretical framework (Galanter for generative art, Russell for emotion, Berlyne for empirical aesthetics, Helmholtz-Gibson-Bayesian for perception), **survey alternative framings explicitly before settling on one**. Don't claim canonicity for the first framework I happened to recall.

**Why:** User caught (2026-05-17) that the Algorithmic Composition + Tools sweep anchored on Galanter's 2003 "What is Generative Art?" as "the canonical theoretical anchor of contemporary generative art" without surveying alternatives. The claim is **overstated**. Alternatives I didn't survey: Boden's computational creativity (treated as sub-concept; could have been root); Whitelaw's artificial-life / emergence framing; practice-led / studio-research lineage (Reas, Hobbs, Hoff, Sagmeister); procedural-generation game-dev framing; postdigital aesthetics (Cramer, Manovich); live-coding (McLean, Jack); AI-art / latent-space (Hertzmann, Klingemann); on-chain generative (Hobbs, Cherniak). For the wiki's four priorities, different framings foreground different things — parametric-identity studio-research framing fits branding better than Galanter's complexity-aesthetic theory.

Same risk applies retroactively to Affect Foundations (did I oversell Russell circumplex canonicity? Did I oversell Berlyne canonicity?) and L1 Cleanup (did I oversell the Bayesian-predictive Helmholtz-Gibson synthesis?). User has proposed (Option C, Phase 3) a targeted audit of these.

**How to apply:**

- At the **start** of any depth-dive that anchors on a framework: explicitly list alternative framings. Even a quick "alternatives to consider: X, Y, Z" demonstrates the survey happened. autoresearch is appropriate here.
- In the **synthesis page**: explicitly position the chosen framing as *one defensible framing*, not THE framing. Note its competitors and what they foreground differently.
- Pay particular attention to claims of unification (e.g., "Galanter's effective complexity = Berlyne's arousal-potential, the wiki's central theoretical pillar"). Unification claims are seductive but often overstate consensus.
- Empirical claims should be **flagged as contested** if they are — the complexity-preference inverted-U is not as clean as the wiki currently presents it.
