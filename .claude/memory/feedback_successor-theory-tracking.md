When the wiki relies on a theoretical framework, the page must distinguish between:

- A **live contemporary framework** — actively defended, generative of current research
- A **useful historical anchor** — pedagogically valuable but substantially superseded by named successor theories

When citing work from primary sources predating ~2010 in fast-moving fields (empirical aesthetics, emotion theory, perception, cognitive psychology), search explicitly for the successor literature *before* framing the original as current.

**Why:** Phase 3 audit (2026-05-17) caught the wiki presenting [[Berlyne's Arousal-Potential Theory|Berlyne's 1971 framework]] as its contemporary theoretical workhorse and as the wiki's "central theoretical pillar." Per the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's *Empirical Aesthetics* entry: "The results of testing Berlyne's arousal theory of aesthetic appreciation have been mixed at best and therefore the theory has been mostly abandoned." The successor literature (named explicitly): [[Processing Fluency Theory|processing fluency]] (Reber et al. 2004), contemporary neuroaesthetics (Vessel, Leder & Nadal), and predictive-processing accounts (Van de Cruys 2017). The wiki had pages on the successors but presented them as Berlyne's *continuation* rather than its *replacement*.

The error was subtle: every individual page was defensible. But the *aggregate framing* — Berlyne as the wiki's theoretical workhorse — was no longer empirically supportable. The successor theories carry the contemporary load; Berlyne carries historical-anchor load.

**How to apply:**

- When relying on a framework cited primarily from work pre-2010, search the literature for the successor theory. Phrases that work: "X replaced", "X successor", "X abandoned", "post-X", "limitations of X", "X has been criticized".
- If a named successor theory exists in the wiki already (e.g., processing fluency exists as a page), explicitly position the original framework as historical infrastructure with a "Successor theories" callout and cross-link.
- If the successor exists but the wiki doesn't have a page yet, the page is high-priority to write.
- Don't conflate "still pedagogically useful" with "still contemporary live framework." Both can be true, but the wiki should say which.
- This applies retroactively too — any old page mentioning a pre-2010 framework as the *current* approach should be re-audited.

Specific examples from the Phase 3 audit:

| Historical framework | Status | Named successor |
|---|---|---|
| Berlyne arousal-potential (1971) | Substantially abandoned | Processing fluency (Reber 2004), neuroaesthetics (Vessel) |
| Russell circumplex (1980) | Useful representation, not universal | Constructionist (Barrett 2017) — alternative not strict successor |
| Helmholtz inferentialism (1867) | Contested live framing | Bayesian / predictive-processing (one defensible reading) |
| Ekman basic emotions (1960s+) | Strong form contested | Barrett constructionism + Crivelli-Fridlund behavioral ecology view |
