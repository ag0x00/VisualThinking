Every new art/design generation direction goes through a learning sequence — not straight to code:

1. **Read the wiki** for what we already know on the topic (concepts, techniques, prior research). Lead with this; cite it.
2. **Autoresearch a deeper, project-specific dive** — but ONLY where the wiki is thin. Don't redundantly re-research what's already filed (e.g. the IGP library landscape is already in `wiki/questions/Research - IGP Library Landscape 2026-05-18.md`).
3. **Find what "good" commonly looks like** in that domain — conventions, exemplars, what practitioners actually value.
4. **Locate or adapt publicly available code/libraries** — don't reinvent (see [[npm-audit-before-design]]); the wiki is where we point to that prior art.
5. **Tease out the specifics in conversation with the user.**

**Why:** the user explicitly does NOT expect to pre-think every method for making a design engaging or compelling — *that is what the wiki is for.* When a generated design feels like "something is missing," the expected behavior is to consult the wiki (e.g. Gestalt closure / amodal completion, directed tension, harmony, rhythm) and bring concrete new ideas and suggestions INTO the conversation, rather than guessing or asking the user to supply them. The wiki is a participant in the generation loop, not a passive reference. (Surfaced 2026-05-21 when discussing broken-but-completable Truchet lines — the user noted "this is the part where I was planning our wiki to become invaluable.")

**How to apply:** when the user opens a new art direction, open by synthesizing what the wiki/MCP + prior research already give us (named, cited), fill genuine gaps with targeted autoresearch, surface "what good looks like," point at adaptable public code, then brainstorm specifics. Keep it minimal — don't re-derive what's filed. Relates to [[cross-domain-composition]], [[test-artifact-vs-workflow]], [[npm-audit-before-design]].
