# PAD Emotion Model

**Mehrabian and Russell's three-dimensional emotion model** (1974, refined 1996): every emotional state is a point in $(P, A, D)$ — **Pleasure × Arousal × Dominance**. Extends [[Russell's Affect Circumplex]] by adding the **dominance** axis, which Russell's 2D model collapses.

The PAD model is the most-used dimensional emotion model in **advertising and environmental psychology** because it captures the "in-control vs overwhelmed" distinction that's critical for retail, ambient-design, and brand contexts.

## The three dimensions

| Dimension | Range | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **P — Pleasure** | $-1$ (displeasing) $\to +1$ (pleasing) | Same as Russell's valence |
| **A — Arousal** | $-1$ (sleepy) $\to +1$ (excited) | Same as Russell's arousal |
| **D — Dominance** | $-1$ (submissive, overwhelmed) $\to +1$ (dominant, in control) | The added third axis |

The (P, A) plane is the Russell circumplex; PAD adds D as the *vertical* axis through it.

## Why dominance matters

Two emotions can share (P, A) coordinates but differ on dominance:

- **Anger** = (–, +, +) — negative valence, high arousal, **high dominance** (the angry person feels powerful, ready to act).
- **Fear** = (–, +, –) — negative valence, high arousal, **low dominance** (the fearful person feels overwhelmed, ready to flee).

In Russell's 2D plane these are nearly identical points. PAD distinguishes them on the action-tendency axis: **anger pushes outward (approach); fear pulls inward (avoid)**.

Similarly:

- **Pride** vs **happiness**: both positive valence, mid-high arousal — but pride has high dominance, happiness has neutral.
- **Boredom** vs **disgust**: both negative valence, low-mid arousal — but boredom has neutral dominance, disgust has high (rejection is an *active* response).

This is the *approach/avoidance* axis that pure-valence models can't see.

## Mehrabian's PAD-emotion mappings

Eight prototypical emotions with PAD coordinates (Mehrabian 1996; values normalized $[-1, +1]$):

| Emotion | P | A | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exuberant** | +.4 | +.5 | +.6 |
| **Dependent** | +.5 | +.5 | –.6 |
| **Relaxed** | +.6 | –.4 | +.4 |
| **Docile** | +.4 | –.4 | –.5 |
| **Hostile** | –.5 | +.5 | +.5 |
| **Anxious** | –.5 | +.4 | –.7 |
| **Disdainful** | –.4 | –.5 | +.4 |
| **Bored** | –.4 | –.5 | –.4 |

These are *prototypes*; specific emotion words land near (not exactly at) these anchors.

## Connection to environmental and brand applications

PAD was developed in part for **environmental psychology** — how environments make us feel, and how those feelings drive behavior. Donovan & Rossiter (1982) showed that **retail environments** affect approach-vs-avoidance behavior (time spent, money spent) through the PAD dimensions:

- High-P environments increase positive behavior (return visits, recommendation).
- High-A environments amplify whatever direction P points.
- High-D environments encourage exploration; low-D environments encourage either passive acceptance or escape.

This is why "luxury" stores often run **high P, moderate A, very high D** (calm, controlled, you-belong-here) while **clearance / fast-fashion** runs **moderate P, very high A, moderate D** (energetic, urgent, action-oriented).

Same logic applies to brand-identity systems: brand "voice" decomposes into PAD coordinates and the visual language realizes them.

## PAD in advertising and packaging

- Aaker's Brand Personality Scale (sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness) maps approximately to PAD regions:
  - *Sincerity* = high P, low-mid A, low D
  - *Excitement* = high P, high A, mid D
  - *Competence* = mid P, mid A, high D
  - *Sophistication* = high P, low A, high D
  - *Ruggedness* = mid P, mid A, very high D
- This means the same Plutchik or Russell coordinate maps to **different brand emotions** depending on the D position.

## Caveats and what's contested

- The **dominance dimension is the least-replicated** of the three across studies. Some meta-analyses (Bradley & Lang 1994 with IAPS images) find D explains ~5–10% of self-rating variance, while P explains ~50% and A explains ~30%. PAD's dominance is **real but small**.
- The **collapse to 2D** (PA) is often *good enough* for visual / musical emotion work; D becomes essential when the stimulus involves **agency, social hierarchy, or behavioral action tendency**.
- For **purely visual abstract stimuli** (a generative-art image with no figures), dominance is harder to ground than for figural or environmental stimuli.

## Why this matters for the wiki's four priorities

| Priority | Use |
|---|---|
| 1. Generative art | Less direct; dominance is harder to encode in pure abstract form, easier in figurative scenes. Useful for character / scene generators. |
| 2. Branding | **Critical.** Brand "personality" (competent / sophisticated / rugged / etc.) is largely about dominance. PAD lets brands be specified precisely. |
| 3. Graphic design | Editorial / advertising tone often turns on D (authoritative vs friendly; in-control vs urgent). |
| 4. Music-reactive visualizers | Music's D axis (commanding vs vulnerable; assertive bass vs delicate piano) maps to visual D (large dominant masses vs small vulnerable forms). |

## Mapping PAD to Arnheim's vocabulary

| PAD axis | Arnheim structural primitive |
|---|---|
| **P** (pleasure) | Harmony vs discord; conformance vs struggle; rising vs falling |
| **A** (arousal) | Directed-tension magnitude (sum of obliqueness, asymmetry, gradient) |
| **D** (dominance) | Expansion vs contraction; approach vs withdrawal; mass scale; figure-ground prominence; **center vs periphery placement** in the [[The Structural Skeleton|structural skeleton]] |

Dominance maps to **scale + position + expansion**: a large, centered, expanded form reads as dominant; a small, peripheral, contracted form reads as submissive. This is **directly programmable** as a layout-and-mass control.
