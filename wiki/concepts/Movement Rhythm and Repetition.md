**Field stub from catalog sweep 2026-05-17, now depth-dived. Substantially pre-covered by Arnheim Sweep 3.**

How **patterns of repetition with variation** create visual rhythm — the design discipline beneath grids, friezes, ornament, tessellation, and most music-reactive visual systems. The "movement" here is the *eye's traversal* of repeated structure, not physical motion (that's [[Stroboscopic Motion]], [[Phenomenal Causality]], etc.).

Most of the perceptual substrate for this field was covered in Arnheim Sweep 3. This stub names what's *still* missing: the **design-discipline catalog** of rhythm types, the **ornament / Islamic geometry / textile** tradition, and **algorithmic rhythm-generation** practice.

## Canonical figures and traditions

- **Owen Jones** — *The Grammar of Ornament* (1856). The single most-influential survey of cross-cultural visual rhythm. Egyptian, Greek, Moorish, Celtic, Chinese, Indian, "savage tribes."
- **William Morris** — Arts and Crafts; pattern design (wallpaper, textile) as principled rhythmic composition.
- **Islamic geometric pattern tradition** — most-developed surface-rhythm tradition in human history. Penrose-tile precursors centuries before Penrose.
- **M. C. Escher** — Tessellations and impossible figures. Rhythm via congruent-tile rule plus identity-breaking transformations.
- **H. S. M. Coxeter** — *Introduction to Geometry* (1961), regular polytopes; the math of tessellation.
- **Roger Penrose & Robert Ammann** — aperiodic tiling (1974). Pure mathematics that anticipates quasicrystal physics.
- **Bridget Riley** — Op-art (1960s+); the modern Western canonical figure for rhythmic-pattern as fine art.

## Key concepts (depth-dive will expand)

### Rhythm types in 2D design

- **Translational symmetry** (rule-of-thirds repetition; the 7 frieze groups in mathematics).
- **Reflectional symmetry** (mirror axes).
- **Rotational symmetry** (point-group rotations).
- **Glide reflection** (translation + reflection).
- **All combined** in the 17 wallpaper groups (Fedorov 1891; mathematical exhaustive enumeration of 2D periodic patterns).
- **Aperiodic / quasi-periodic** (Penrose tiles): pattern without translational symmetry; long-range order without repeat.

### Rhythm dynamics

- **Regular rhythm** — exact repetition. Mechanical, see [[Organic vs Mechanical Motion]].
- **Alternating rhythm** — ABABAB; introduces a 2-element cycle.
- **Progressive rhythm** — repeat with monotonic variation (size, color, angle). Generates [[Directed Tension|directed tension]] via gradient.
- **Random rhythm** — repetition with stochastic variation. The "natural" rhythm of forests, crowds, rain.
- **Flowing rhythm** — repetition along a curve or path.

### Repetition with variation

- The phrase is Anne Lamott's writing advice but the principle is universal. **Pure repetition** (mechanical) → boring; **pure variation** (chaos) → noise; **repetition-with-variation** is the aesthetic sweet spot. Maps to [[Visual Entropy]] / [[Fractal Dimension]] / Berlyne arousal-potential.
- This is the **same** mid-range preference that the wiki has documented across multiple aesthetic measures. **Rhythm IS the order-vs-complexity tradeoff** expressed in pattern.

### Cross-modal rhythm

- Musical rhythm (beat, meter, tempo) and visual rhythm (spacing, accent, flow) share structure. Reigle, Marchewka, and Eerola 2013 on cross-modal rhythm perception.
- Direct mapping for music-reactive visualizers (priority 4): musical accent → visual accent in pattern; musical syncopation → visual offset from expected position.
