# Western Iconographic Systems

The **major convention-coded symbol systems** of Western visual culture: **Christian iconography** (Marian, Christological, saint attributes), **Greco-Roman mythology** (gods identified by attributes), **heraldry** (combinatorial grammar of arms), **alchemy / astrology / tarot** (occult-symbolic), and the **contemporary brand-iconography** evolution (Apple's apple, Nike's swoosh). Anchored on **James Hall's *Dictionary of Subjects & Symbols in Art*** (1974) as the canonical Western reference.

> [!warning] One iconographic tradition among many
> This page covers **Western** iconographic systems specifically. [[Non-Western Iconographic Systems]] covers Buddhist, Hindu, CJK, Islamic, and other traditions. Per [[Wiki Methodology|convention #5]] (cross-cultural validity), the wiki must avoid treating Western iconography as default or universal — see [[Cross-Cultural Color Variation]] and [[Multilingual Typography]] for the same convention applied to color and typography.

## Christian iconography

The deepest historical system in Western visual culture. Developed across ~2,000 years; densely codified.

### Marian symbols (Virgin Mary)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lily / fleur-de-lis** | Purity; Annunciation context |
| **Rose** | Mary as "rose without thorns" |
| **Enclosed garden** (hortus conclusus) | Virginity; Song of Solomon reference |
| **Blue mantle** | Heaven; royalty |
| **Crescent moon under feet** | Immaculate Conception (Revelation 12:1) |
| **12-star crown** | Queen of Heaven |
| **Heart (often pierced)** | Sorrows of Mary |

### Christological symbols (Christ)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lamb** (sometimes with banner) | Sacrifice; "Lamb of God" |
| **Pelican** | Self-sacrifice (legend of pelican feeding young from own breast) |
| **Fish** (ichthys) | "Jesus Christ, God's Son, Savior" acronym |
| **IHS / IHC monogram** | First three letters of Jesus in Greek |
| **Chi-Rho (☧)** | First two letters of Christ in Greek |
| **Cross variants** | Latin (long vertical), Greek (equal arms), Tau (T-shape), Celtic (circle), Anchor cross |
| **Pantokrator gesture** | Right hand raised, two-fingered blessing |
| **Sacred Heart** | Devotion to Christ's love |

### Saint attributes (identifying objects)

Saints are identified by their *attributes* — objects held or accompanying:

| Saint | Attribute(s) |
|---|---|
| Peter | Keys (heaven), inverted cross (martyrdom) |
| Paul | Sword, book |
| John the Evangelist | Eagle, chalice with serpent |
| John the Baptist | Camel-hair, lamb, head on platter |
| Catherine of Alexandria | Wheel, palm of martyrdom |
| Sebastian | Arrows piercing body |
| Lawrence | Grill (martyrdom instrument) |
| Christopher | Christ-child on shoulder, river crossing |
| Francis of Assisi | Birds, stigmata |
| George | Dragon, armor |
| Anthony of Padua | Christ-child, lily |
| Mary Magdalene | Long hair, jar of ointment, skull |

The attributes are the *recognition mechanism*: knowing the dictionary lets the viewer identify the figure even without naming.

### Numbers and colors in Christian iconography

- **3**: Trinity (Father / Son / Spirit; or three Magi, three temptations)
- **4**: Evangelists (Matthew=man, Mark=lion, Luke=ox, John=eagle); cardinal virtues
- **7**: Sacraments, deadly sins, virtues, sorrows, gifts of the Spirit
- **12**: Apostles, tribes of Israel
- **Gold**: divine light / heaven
- **Blue**: heaven; Mary's color
- **Red**: martyrdom; Holy Spirit; Christ's blood
- **White**: purity; resurrection
- **Black**: mourning; humility (monastic)
- **Purple**: penitence; royalty
- **Green**: hope; Trinity (older symbolism)

## Greco-Roman mythology

The second major Western iconographic system, revived in the Renaissance and continuing through Neoclassicism.

### Gods identified by attributes

| God | Attributes |
|---|---|
| **Zeus / Jupiter** | Thunderbolt, eagle, throne, oak |
| **Hera / Juno** | Peacock, pomegranate, diadem |
| **Apollo** | Lyre, laurel, sun, bow, Muses |
| **Artemis / Diana** | Crescent moon, bow, deer, hounds |
| **Athena / Minerva** | Owl, aegis (shield with Medusa head), spear, olive |
| **Hermes / Mercury** | Caduceus, winged sandals, winged helmet |
| **Aphrodite / Venus** | Doves, roses, scallop shell, mirror |
| **Ares / Mars** | Sword, helmet, armor |
| **Hephaestus / Vulcan** | Hammer, anvil, lame leg |
| **Dionysus / Bacchus** | Thyrsus (pinecone-staff), grapes, panther, ivy |
| **Poseidon / Neptune** | Trident, dolphin, horses |
| **Hades / Pluto** | Bident, Cerberus, pomegranate |

### Heroes and Muses

Greco-Roman heroes (Heracles, Theseus, Perseus, Odysseus) and the nine Muses (Calliope, Clio, Erato, Euterpe, Melpomene, Polyhymnia, Terpsichore, Thalia, Urania) have similar attribute-based identification.

### Allegorical figures

Personifications appear throughout Renaissance and Baroque art:

- **Virtues**: Faith, Hope, Charity, Justice, Prudence, Fortitude, Temperance — each with specific attributes
- **Liberal arts**: Grammar, Rhetoric, Logic (trivium); Arithmetic, Geometry, Music, Astronomy (quadrivium)
- **Seasons**, **Continents**, **Times of Day**, **Five Senses** — all conventional personifications

## Heraldry

**Heraldry** is one of the most-systematic Western iconographic systems — a strict combinatorial grammar for visual identity, developed ~12th-15th centuries.

### Components

- **Tinctures**: limited palette
  - **Metals**: Or (gold/yellow), Argent (silver/white)
  - **Colors**: Gules (red), Azure (blue), Sable (black), Vert (green), Purpure (purple), Tenné (orange), Sanguine (blood-red)
  - **Furs**: Ermine, Vair (and variants)
- **Ordinaries**: basic geometric divisions (chief, pale, fess, bend, chevron, cross, saltire)
- **Charges**: heraldic figures (lion, eagle, fleur-de-lis, rose, star, crescent, mullet, etc.)
- **Attitudes**: how a charge is posed (lion rampant, passant, couchant, statant, dormant)

### The "rule of tincture"

Metal-on-color or color-on-metal only; not metal-on-metal or color-on-color. The rule maximizes readability and is one of the earliest *visual-accessibility* design principles.

For contemporary branding: heraldic logic still appears in sports-team identity, university crests, brewery logos, military / governmental marks. The combinatorial grammar makes heraldry a *generative system* by design.

## Occult iconography

### Tarot

78-card deck. 22 **Major Arcana** (Fool, Magician, High Priestess, Empress, Emperor, Hierophant, Lovers, Chariot, Strength, Hermit, Wheel of Fortune, Justice, Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, Devil, Tower, Star, Moon, Sun, Judgement, World) + 56 **Minor Arcana** (Wands, Cups, Swords, Pentacles, each with 14 cards). Massive 19th-20th century influence on Western occultism; substantial influence on contemporary design (especially Aleister-Crowley-Thoth lineage).

### Astrology

12 zodiac signs (Aries, Taurus, ..., Pisces) + 10 planetary bodies (Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, plus outer Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) + 12 astrological houses. Pre-Copernican model; symbolic for >2000 years. Appears throughout Renaissance, Romantic, and contemporary visual culture.

### Alchemy

Each metal has a glyph (☉ gold, ☽ silver, ☿ mercury, ♀ copper, ♂ iron, ♃ tin, ♄ lead). Processes (calcination, dissolution, separation, conjunction, fermentation, distillation, coagulation) have symbols. Jung's archetype theory ([[Jungian Archetypes and Brand Archetypes]]) drew heavily from alchemical symbolism.

## Contemporary Western brand iconography

Logos as pure-symbol signs (per Peirce: convention-only meaning, no resemblance):

- **Apple** — silhouette of apple with bite. Pure convention; no apple-related meaning.
- **Nike** — abstract swoosh. Originally referenced Greek goddess Nike but the swoosh resembles nothing literal.
- **McDonald's** — golden arches. M-shape; visual coincidence with letter-form.
- **Mercedes-Benz** — three-pointed star. Originally land/sea/air; now pure-symbol.
- **Coca-Cola** — Spencerian script. Type *is* the logo.
- **Target** — bullseye. Iconic + symbolic.

Brand iconography emerges from heraldic / Christian / Greco-Roman lineages but operates *pure-conventionally* — the symbol means what custom has *trained* viewers to recognize, not what its form depicts.
