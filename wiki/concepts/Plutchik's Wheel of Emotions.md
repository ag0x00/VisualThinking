# Plutchik's Wheel of Emotions

**Robert Plutchik's 1980 categorical-evolutionary model**: 8 primary emotions arranged in 4 polar pairs around a wheel, with **dyadic compounds** at the intersections (e.g., joy + trust = love). An evolutionary-functional framework — each primary emotion is tied to an adaptive behavior — and one of the most-cited categorical models, alongside Ekman's basic-6.

Where [[Russell's Affect Circumplex]] is the **continuous dimensional** account, Plutchik is the **discrete categorical** account. They're complementary, not competing: Plutchik's 8 anchors map cleanly onto Russell's plane.

## The eight primary emotions

Arranged in 4 polar pairs (across-the-wheel antagonists):

| Polar pair | Adaptive function |
|---|---|
| **Joy ↔ Sadness** | Approach/connect ↔ withdraw/grieve |
| **Trust ↔ Disgust** | Affiliate ↔ reject |
| **Fear ↔ Anger** | Avoid threat ↔ destroy threat |
| **Surprise ↔ Anticipation** | Reorient ↔ predict/prepare |

Each emotion is a *functional category* — Plutchik's claim is that they correspond to discrete behavior systems with evolutionary roots (Darwin lineage, see [[Emotion Psychology]]).

## The three-layer wheel structure

Plutchik visualized this as a **3D cone** flattened to a wheel:

1. **Intensity axis (vertical / radial)**: each primary emotion has a graded intensity. *Ecstasy → joy → serenity* is the joy axis. *Rage → anger → annoyance*. *Terror → fear → apprehension*. Most-intense at the center; least-intense at the rim.
2. **Angular position**: the 8 primaries are at fixed angles, polar pairs across.
3. **Dyadic combinations**: at each intersection between primaries, a *dyad* — a combined emotion that is functionally the union of its parents.

## The eight primary dyads

The first-order combinations (adjacent primaries):

| Primary 1 | Primary 2 | Dyad |
|---|---|---|
| Joy | Trust | **Love** |
| Trust | Fear | **Submission** |
| Fear | Surprise | **Awe** |
| Surprise | Sadness | **Disapproval** |
| Sadness | Disgust | **Remorse** |
| Disgust | Anger | **Contempt** |
| Anger | Anticipation | **Aggressiveness** |
| Anticipation | Joy | **Optimism** |

Second-order dyads (primaries 2 apart): pride, fatalism, etc. Third-order dyads (3 apart): rare-named compounds. The full table is in Plutchik 1980.

## Each primary has graded intensity

The same emotion at three intensities (from Plutchik's original wheel):

| Most intense | Mid | Least intense |
|---|---|---|
| Ecstasy | Joy | Serenity |
| Admiration | Trust | Acceptance |
| Terror | Fear | Apprehension |
| Amazement | Surprise | Distraction |
| Grief | Sadness | Pensiveness |
| Loathing | Disgust | Boredom |
| Rage | Anger | Annoyance |
| Vigilance | Anticipation | Interest |

This 8 × 3 = 24-cell grid is the practical operationalization of the wheel. Plus the 8 first-order dyads = 32 named emotional states.

## Plutchik's psycho-evolutionary postulates

Underpinning the model (from Plutchik 1980):

1. Emotions have an evolutionary history; they are conservative across species.
2. There is a small set of *primary* emotions; all others are combinations or graduations.
3. Emotions are *functional* — they trigger adaptive behavior.
4. Emotions can be paired as opposites (polar structure).
5. Emotions vary in degree (intensity axis).
6. Emotions vary in similarity (angular distance on the wheel).

## Mapping to Russell's circumplex

Plutchik's 8 primaries can be approximately located on the Russell (valence, arousal) plane:

| Plutchik emotion | Approximate Russell location |
|---|---|
| Joy | + valence, mid arousal |
| Trust | + valence, low arousal |
| Anticipation | mid valence, + arousal |
| Surprise | mid valence, ++ arousal |
| Fear | – valence, ++ arousal |
| Anger | – valence, + arousal |
| Disgust | – valence, mid arousal |
| Sadness | – valence, – arousal |

This is the **dimensional-categorical bridge**: a Plutchik label is a *region* of Russell's plane.

## Caveats

- The 8 primaries are **stipulative**, not empirically forced. Other categorical models choose 6 (Ekman) or 4 or 10. Plutchik's specific choice is theoretically motivated (evolutionary-functional) but not the only valid carving.
- The polar-pair structure assumes opposites are *symmetric* on a single axis. This is contestable — fear and anger both share negativity, and their "opposition" is on a specific axis (avoid vs approach) that doesn't generalize.
- The dyads are **descriptive** more than predictive — "joy + trust = love" is plausible but not derived. Treat dyad labels as a vocabulary, not a calculus.
- Cross-cultural validation of the 8 primaries is weaker than for Russell's 2D structure.
