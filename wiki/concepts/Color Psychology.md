# Color Psychology

**Field overview. Catalog sweep 2026-05-17; depth-dive complete same day as part of [[Research - Affect Foundations Sweep]].**

Distinct from colorimetry ([[The Munsell and CIELAB Color Systems]], [[OKLCH]], [[CIEDE2000]]): how colors are **perceived emotionally, culturally, and behaviorally** — what they mean, what they trigger, how associations form, vary, and propagate. Where Arnheim ([[Warm and Cool Colors]], [[Arnheim's Color Syntax]]) supplies the *structural* account, color psychology supplies the *empirical* and *cultural* one.

## Canonical figures

- **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** — *Zur Farbenlehre* (1810). Polemic against Newton; sensual / phenomenal account of color emotion; "yellow … lively, soothing, has a serene gay quality; blue … cold, melancholy."
- **Wassily Kandinsky** — *Concerning the Spiritual in Art* (1911); systematic color-meaning vocabulary tied to spiritual/musical analogies.
- **Faber Birren** — *Color Psychology and Color Therapy* (1950, multiple revisions); industrial / commercial color-emotion mapping; defined 20th-century branding color use.
- **Stephen Palmer & Karen Schloss** (Berkeley) — Ecological Valence Theory (2010): color preferences explained by valence of objects strongly associated with the color. Best empirical model to date.
- **Eva Heller** — *Wie Farben wirken / Psychologie der Farben* (1989, expanded 2000+). Survey-based: 200+ associations across 13 colors for ~2000 respondents (German). Wide secondary citation.

## Key concepts (depth-dive will expand)

- **Ecological Valence Theory** (Palmer & Schloss 2010): people like colors they associate with valenced objects (sky blue good → blue preferred; rotten yellow-green bad → that yellow-green disliked). Most validated empirical account.
- **Cultural variation**: red = luck (China) / danger (US) / mourning (South Africa). White = purity (West) / mourning (East Asia). Universal substrate exists (warm/cool, brightness) but specific color-emotion mappings are heavily cultural.
- **Berlin & Kay color-term universals** (1969): basic color terms emerge in a near-universal order (black/white → red → green/yellow → blue → brown → others). Implies a universal color-category substrate beneath cultural variation.
- **Color-emotion dimensions** (Ou, Luo et al. 2004): empirical study finds 3 emotion factors derived from color stimuli — activity, weight, heat — analogous to PAD-without-pleasure.
- **The Stroop effect** and color-cognition interference: color is processed pre-attentively and influences cognition even when irrelevant to the task.
- **Color preference universals**: blue is most preferred globally (Palmer & Schloss replicated this); yellow-green is least preferred. Probably ecological.
- **Marketing-applied color psychology** (Labrecque & Milne 2012): consistent brand-color effects on perceived brand traits (excitement, competence, sincerity).

## Connection to Arnheim and existing wiki pages

- [[Warm and Cool Colors]] — Arnheim's *structural* deviation theory. Color psychology depth-dive will test it against the *associative* (warmth = fire, blood, sun → warm color category) hypothesis.
- [[Complementary Colors]] — Arnheim's mutual-completion principle. Color psychology adds: cultural-specific complement-pair meanings (red-green = Christmas, etc).
- [[Arnheim's Color Syntax]] — pure structural. Color psychology adds associations onto pairs.
- [[OKLCH]] — perceptual color space. Color psychology adds the next layer up: what perceptually distinct colors *mean*.

## Depth-dive complete

Three concept pages produced (2026-05-17, see [[Research - Affect Foundations Sweep]]):

- **[[Ecological Valence Theory]]** — Palmer & Schloss 2010. Best-validated theory of color preference (WAVE $r \approx 0.89$). Color preference = weighted average of valence of associated objects.
- **[[Goethe and Kandinsky on Color]]** — historical phenomenological vocabulary (warm/cool, expansive/contractive, active/passive). Physically wrong about optics; experientially valuable as vocabulary.
- **[[Cross-Cultural Color Variation]]** — Berlin-Kay universals; cultural-specific overlays. Dimensional ratings travel; categorical labels don't.

Plus cross-cluster: **[[Cross-Modal Emotion Mapping]]** integrates color into the wider (V, A) substrate.

**Key finding from the sweep**: color preference is *associative*, not symbolic-universal. Brand-color conditioning works because brands become the dominant association for their color (EVT mechanism). Cross-cultural design must audit color choices because object-associations differ.
