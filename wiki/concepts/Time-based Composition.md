# Time-based Composition

> [!success] Depth-dive complete 2026-05-17
> See [[Research - Movement-Rhythm-Style-Symbolism Sweep]]. Page-by-page coverage: [[Eisenstein's Montage Theory]] · [[Murch's Six Editing Rules]] · [[McCloud's Panel Transitions and the Infinite Canvas]] · [[Disney Animation Principles]].

**Field stub from catalog sweep 2026-05-17, now depth-dived. Directly serves priorities 1 (dynamic art) and 4 (visualizers).**

The principles of composing **temporally-extended** visual works — film, animation, comics, motion graphics, real-time visualizers. The question: how does the eye + cognitive system construct meaning from *sequences* of static or moving images?

The wiki already covers Arnheim's perceptual-motion vocabulary ([[Stroboscopic Motion]], [[Phenomenal Causality]], [[Frame of Reference for Motion]], [[Organic vs Mechanical Motion]]). This stub adds the **composition-level** discipline: editing, pacing, panel flow, scene structure, kinetic typography.

## Canonical figures and traditions

- **Sergei Eisenstein** — *Film Form* (1949), *The Film Sense* (1942). Montage theory: five types of montage (metric, rhythmic, tonal, overtonal, intellectual). The founding text of editing theory.
- **Dziga Vertov** — Kino-Eye (1920s); film as visual-language construction. *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929).
- **Walter Murch** — *In the Blink of an Eye* (1995). Editor of *Apocalypse Now*; six-rule editing hierarchy (emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, two-dimensional plane, three-dimensional continuity). Working-editor's wisdom.
- **Scott McCloud** — *Understanding Comics* (1993) and *Making Comics* (2006). The closest thing to a Theory of Time-Based Composition for the non-film media. Six panel-to-panel transitions; the gutter as the place where meaning forms.
- **Saul Bass** — Title sequences (*Vertigo*, *Anatomy of a Murder*, *Psycho*) as time-based graphic-design works.
- **Norman McLaren** — Direct-on-film animation; *Lines Horizontal* (1962). Pure abstract animation pioneer.
- **John Whitney Sr.** — *Digital Harmony* (1980); pioneer of computer animation and motion graphics; explicitly musical structure for visual time.

## Key concepts (depth-dive will expand)

### Eisenstein's montage types (depth-dive will detail)

1. **Metric** — cuts on a fixed time interval (every N frames). Mechanical, predictable.
2. **Rhythmic** — cuts driven by content (within-shot action). More organic.
3. **Tonal** — cuts driven by emotional/atmospheric tone matching or contrasting.
4. **Overtonal** — combination of all the above; the "harmonic" total.
5. **Intellectual** — cuts that juxtapose ideas (the Odessa Steps lion-statue sequence). Causes the viewer to draw a conceptual link from juxtaposition.

### Murch's six editing rules (priority order)

1. Emotion
2. Story
3. Rhythm
4. Eye-trace (where attention is in successive shots)
5. Two-dimensional plane (composition across the cut)
6. Three-dimensional continuity (camera position, space)

The hierarchy says: serve emotion first, even at the cost of physical-space continuity. This is *exactly* the [[Physiognomic Perception]] thesis applied to time.

### McCloud's panel transitions (comics specific)

1. **Moment-to-moment** (subtle: one moment to the next).
2. **Action-to-action** (a single subject in distinct actions).
3. **Subject-to-subject** (within a scene; eye jumps).
4. **Scene-to-scene** (significant distance/time).
5. **Aspect-to-aspect** (a single moment, multiple views — common in Japanese manga; rare in Western comics).
6. **Non-sequitur** (no logical relation; rare; creates poetic effect).

The **gutter** (white space between panels) is where the reader does the work of constructing motion + continuity. Closure (gestalt continuation across the gap) is the mechanism.

### Pacing and rhythm

- **Tempo** = cuts per minute. Action films: 1–3 sec/shot. Drama: 5–15 sec. Tarkovsky / Bela Tarr: 60+ sec.
- **Rhythm** = the *pattern* of cut-lengths over time. Constant-pace = mechanical (see [[Organic vs Mechanical Motion]]). Varied-pace = organic, dramatic.
- **Beats and accents**: like music, time-based work has a metric structure; visual accents (high-intensity shots) land on metric strong points.

### Kinetic typography

- Saul Bass's title sequences; Kyle Cooper (Imaginary Forces) post-1995 evolution.
- Type that moves; type that arrives, exits, transforms.
- Type-on-beat for music visualization is the priority-4 case.
