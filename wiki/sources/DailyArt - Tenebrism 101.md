# DailyArt Magazine — Tenebrism 101

## Summary

Focused intro article on [[Tenebrism]] as a lighting technique. Covers the term's origin (*tenebroso* = dark/gloomy), the distinction from [[Chiaroscuro]], canonical examples by Caravaggio (especially *The Calling of Saint Matthew*), and the spread of the technique to Artemisia Gentileschi, the Utrecht Caravaggisti (Gerard van Honthorst), Jusepe de Ribera, and the candlelight tradition (Georges de La Tour).

## What it contributes

- A clean tenebrism-vs-chiaroscuro distinction for non-specialists.
- The "candlelight tradition" framing as a tenebrism sub-genre.
- Pointers to specific paintings: *Judith Slaying Holofernes* (Gentileschi, 1614–21), *The Procuress* (Honthorst, 1625), *Martyrdom of Saint Andrew* (Ribera, 1628), *Penitent Magdalene* (La Tour, c. 1640).

## Key claims

- **high** Tenebrism is characterized by extreme contrast of light and dark to heighten dramatic effect.
- **high** Caravaggio's *Calling of Saint Matthew* (1599–1600) is the canonical tenebrist work; the light beam acts as a spotlight on Christ's hand and Matthew's face.
- **high** Utrecht Caravaggisti were a Dutch artistic group inspired by Caravaggio; they painted genre scenes.
- **high** The candlelight tradition uses a single candle as sole light source, for mood/atmosphere rather than narrative drama.
- **medium** Tenebrism and *caravaggism* are sometimes used interchangeably.
