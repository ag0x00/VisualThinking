# Cramer — What Is 'Post-Digital'?

> Cramer, Florian. *What Is 'Post-Digital'?* **APRJA (A Peer-Reviewed Journal About) 3:1** "Post-Digital Research," Aarhus University, 2014. ISSN 2245-7755. CC BY-NC-SA. Reprinted in David M. Berry & Michael Dieter (eds.), *Postdigital Aesthetics: Art, Computation and Design*, Palgrave Macmillan 2015, pp. 12–26.
>
> Full PDF ingested 2026-05-17 from `~/Downloads/Florian Cramer WHAT IS POST-DIGITAL.pdf`. All quotes below are verbatim from this primary source.

## Why this source is canonical for the wiki

This is the most-cited theoretical treatment of the **post-digital** framing (one of nine in [[Framings of Generative Art]]). Cramer is the principal theorist of the term; the paper is referenced across the field's anthology literature ([[Postdigital Aesthetics|Berry & Dieter 2015]] reprints it as the opening chapter). The wiki uses Cramer as the theoretical anchor of [[Postdigital Aesthetics]].

The paper is shorter, denser, and more analytical than the secondary-source paraphrases suggested. Cramer's argument has at least **five distinct strands**, only the first of which (the glitch / anti-clean strand) was previously captured in the wiki.

## Cramer's central definitional moves

### The simplest definition (p.14, the most-quoted single line)

> "The simplest definition of 'post-digital' describes a media aesthetics which opposes such digital high-tech and high-fidelity cleanness."

But Cramer is explicit (p.15) that this is incomplete:
> "There is a peculiar overlap between on one hand a post-digital rejection of digital high tech, and on the other hand a post-digital rejection of digital low quality."

Vinyl-LP listeners reject CDs *and* MP3s; cassette / typewriter / mechanical-camera revivalists reject both high-tech and low-fi-trash. Glitch aesthetics (Cascone 2000) are themselves "precisely the same kind of digital trash dismissed by 'post-digital' vinyl listeners." The framing is **inconsistent on aesthetic-quality grounds**, which is why Cramer ultimately reframes it on different axes.

### Post-digital as post-disenchantment (p.12)

> "The term 'post-digital' can be used to describe either a contemporary disenchantment with digital information systems and media gadgets, or a period in which our fascination with these systems and gadgets has become historical — just like the dot-com age ultimately became historical in the 2013 novels of Thomas Pynchon and Dave Eggers."

The historical hinge is **Snowden / NSA disclosures** (post-2013): "this disenchantment has quickly grown from a niche 'hipster' phenomenon to a mainstream position — one which is likely to have a serious impact on all cultural and business practices based on networked electronic devices and Internet services."

### Post-digital as post-colonial, NOT post-historical (p.13)

> "Postcolonialism does not in any way mean an end of colonialism (akin to Hegel's and Fukuyama's 'end of history'), but rather its mutation into new power structures, less obvious but no less pervasive, which have a profound and lasting impact on languages and cultures, and most significantly continue to govern geopolitics and global production chains. In this sense, the post-digital condition is a post-apocalyptic one: the state of affairs after the initial upheaval caused by the computerisation and global digital networking of communication, technical infrastructures, markets and geopolitics."

Crucial: "It is an objective fact that the age in which we now live is *not* a post-digital age, neither in terms of technological developments — with no end in sight to the trend towards further digitisation and computerisation."

The "post-" in post-digital should be read as in post-punk, post-feminism, post-communism — *mutation*, not termination.

### Post-digital as against-the-universal-machine (p.16)

This is the framing's deepest analytical claim — and was absent from the wiki before this ingest:

> "Proponents of 'post-digital' attitudes may reject digital technology as either sterile high tech or low-fidelity trash. In both cases, they dismiss the idea of digital processing as the sole universal all-purpose form of information processing. Consequently, they also dismiss the notion of the computer as the universal machine, and the notion of digital computational devices as all-purpose media."

The argument resonates with the [[Live Coding and Algorave|TOPLAP]] insistence on the specificity of code-as-medium (against opaque-tool framing) and with the [[Practice-led Studio Research|practice-led]] preference for medium-specific making.

### Post-digital as anti-'new media' / hybrids of old + new (pp.17–18)

> "'Post-digital' describes a perspective on digital information technology which no longer focuses on technical innovation or improvement, but instead rejects the kind of techno-positivist innovation narratives exemplified by media such as *Wired* magazine, Ray Kurzweil's Google-sponsored 'singularity' movement, and of course Silicon Valley."

The DIY framing (p.19) — Cramer's own preferred reframing:
> "When hacker-style and community-centric working methods are no longer specific to 'digital' culture (since they are now just as likely to be found at an 'analog' zine fair as in a 'digital' computer lab), then the established dichotomy of 'old' and 'new' media — as synonymous in practice with 'analog' and 'digital' — becomes obsolete, making way for a new differentiation: **one between shrink-wrapped culture and do-it-yourself culture**."

The 1990s assumption (mass media = corporate, new media = DIY) is inverted now that user-generated content has been co-opted into corporate social media. So the *real* distinction is DIY vs corporate, not old vs new.

### Semiotic shift from symbolic to indexical (p.19)

> "The 'maker movement' — as manifested in fab labs, but also at zine fairs — represents a shift from the symbolic, as the preferred semiotic mode of digital systems (and of which the login is the perfect example), toward the indexical: from code to traces, and from text to context. ... The majority of today's zines and experimental Super 8 films, however, tend to focus less on content and more on pure materiality, so that the medium, such as paper or celluloid, is indeed the message — a shift from semantics to pragmatics, and from metaphysics to ontology."

This is a *concrete* claim about the aesthetic axis post-digital work occupies: emphasis on **material trace** (paper grain, vinyl crackle, celluloid scratch) over **encoded symbol**. A useful computable handle for art-system design: emphasize indexical features (texture, grain, artefact) over symbolic ones (legible glyph, clean shape).

### "Technically, there is no such thing as 'digital media'" (p.20)

> "Media, in the technical sense of storage, transmission, computation and display devices, are always analog. ... anything aesthetic (in the literal sense of *aisthesis*, perception) is, by strict technical definition, analog."

The Kittler reference ("There is no software") and a sustained passage on the analog substrate of all electronic computation. Implication: the "digital / analog" dichotomy is a *colloquial* distinction, not a technical-scientific one. Cramer concludes (p.20): "A 'digital artwork' based on the strictly technical definition of 'digital' would most likely be considered 'post-digital' or even 'retro analog' by art curators and humanities scholars."

### Closing — the systems crisis (pp.21–22)

> "And yet, post-digital subculture, whether in Detroit, Rotterdam or elsewhere, is on a fundamental level not so different from such mainstream Silicon Valley utopias. ... The Quantified Self movement, for example, is based on a fiction of agency over one's own body. The entire concept of DIY, whether non-digital, digital or post-digital, is based on the fiction of agency implied by the very notion of the self-made."
>
> "Each of these extremes is, in its own way, symptomatic of a *systems crisis* — not a crisis of this or that system, but rather a crisis of the very paradigm of 'system', as defined by General Systems Theory, itself an offshoot of cybernetics."

Cramer is *self-critical* about the framing. Post-digital is not a stable settled aesthetic but symptomatic of a deeper crisis in systems-theoretical thinking — and the term itself is "ultimately rooted in systems theory" and so cannot escape what it diagnoses.

## Key clarifications Cramer makes

Three sub-arguments that read as side-discursions but matter for wiki accuracy:

1. **Digital ≠ binary; digital ≠ electronic.** "Something can very well be 'digital' without being electronic, and without involving binary zeroes and ones." Examples: Roman alphabet, Gutenberg movable type, piano keys, Western musical notation, floor mosaics. The mechanical typewriter is a digital writing system (countable discrete glyphs).

2. **Analog ≠ non-computational.** "There are also analog computers" (water-and-cups computing additions). Analog signals are continuous; what matters is "correspondence (analogy) with the original physical phenomenon."

3. **Cinema film is digital–analog hybrid.** Each frame's emulsion is analog (undifferentiated particles); the frame strip is digital (discrete, countable). Cramer uses this as a generalizable lesson: most media are not categorically one or the other.

## The Hermlin typewriter-meme case study (pp.10–11, 21)

Cramer opens and closes with **C.D. Hermlin** (the "typewriter hipster" who became "An Object Of Internet Ridicule" in 2013). Hermlin actually earned his living selling custom-written stories from a park bench; the typewriter was the *pragmatically correct* tool (legible output, no batteries, weather-resilient, looks professional). Cramer:
> "This is a perfect example of a post-digital choice: using the technology most suitable to the job, rather than automatically 'defaulting' to the latest 'new media' device."

The case study reframes post-digital not as nostalgia but as **medium-specific pragmatism** — a position much closer to [[Practice-led Studio Research|studio research]] than to Luddite withdrawal.

## What this source does *not* address

- **Specific art practices** beyond zines, vinyl, cassette, typewriter, mechanical photography. The framing is mostly demonstrated through music, publishing, and youth subculture rather than visual / fine art. Glitch art is referenced but not extensively analyzed.
- **AI / generative-art / latent-space**. The paper predates the diffusion-era AI-art boom. Cramer's framework would presumably classify post-2020 AI-art as continuous with the "techno-positivist Silicon Valley" he opposes, but he doesn't say so.
- **Concrete computable handles**. Cramer is a theorist; he describes aesthetic and cultural positions, not algorithms. The semiotic-shift-to-indexical claim is the closest the paper comes to actionable computable specification.

## Reception

- The paper is the opening chapter of Berry & Dieter (2015) *Postdigital Aesthetics* and is reprinted/translated multiple times.
- Cramer revisits the term seven years later in *Postdigital: A Term That Sucks but Is Useful* (with Petar Jandrić, Postdigital Science and Education, March 2021) — the self-critical reflection foreshadowed by this paper's closing.
- The Centre for Postdigital Cultures at Coventry University (est. 2018-02-07, Dir. Gary Hall) institutionalizes the framing this paper anchors.
