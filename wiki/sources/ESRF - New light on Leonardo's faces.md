# ESRF — New light on Leonardo Da Vinci's faces

## Summary

Press release for a peer-reviewed study (*Angewandte Chemie International Edition*, 2010) in which Philippe Walter's team at LC2RMF, with ESRF data analysis, performed non-destructive X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy on **seven Leonardo da Vinci paintings** in the Louvre — including *Mona Lisa* — and analysed **nine faces** to determine the chemical composition and layer thickness of his [[Sfumato]] technique.

## What it contributes

The first direct, quantitative chemical measurement of the layers Leonardo painted, taken without removing samples from the canvas. Confirms several long-standing art-historical claims about sfumato with actual numbers.

## Key claims

- **high** XRF was performed in situ at the Louvre on seven Leonardo paintings: *Virgin of the Rocks*, *Mona Lisa*, *Saint John the Baptist*, *Annunciation*, *Bacchus*, *La Belle Ferronnière*, and *Saint Anne, the Virgin, and the Child*.
- **high** Glaze layers measured at **1–2 micrometers** thick individually.
- **high** Total stacked glaze thickness was **30–40 micrometers** for face shadows.
- **high** Leonardo varied his "shadow recipe" across his 40-year career; light vs. shadow zones use different layering strategies.
- **high** Glazes are translucent, organic-rich layers with low pigment content; the cumulative effect creates depth and volume optically rather than physically.

## Confidence notes

## Why we cite it

Primary scientific evidence for the technical specifics of sfumato. Any computational model of "Leonardo-style" tonal transitions must account for the layered-optical-mixing mechanism this paper documents, not just edge-blur.
