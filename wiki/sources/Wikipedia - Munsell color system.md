# Wikipedia — Munsell color system

## Summary

Reference article on the **Munsell color system**, the early-20th-century perceptual color system that established Hue, Value, and Chroma as independent perceptual dimensions. Covers the history (Albert Munsell, 1898–1905), the system's geometry (irregular three-dimensional color solid), its mathematical specification (H V/C notation), and its continued use today in soil science, forensic pathology, dentistry, brewing, and as a reference for computer color-vision models.

## What it contributes

- The canonical definition of Munsell's three-dimensional system with primary/intermediate hue layout.
- The 100-hue specification (5 principal + 5 intermediate, each subdivided into 10 steps).
- The **non-uniform achievable-color solid** explanation: different hue-value combinations have different chroma ceilings.
- The historical context: Tobias Mayer (1758), Lambert (1772), Runge (1810), Helmholtz (1860) — earlier 3D color models that Munsell improved on by being the first based on **measured human visual responses**.
- The line of descent from Munsell through the Optical Society of America's 1940s renotations to **CIELAB** (1976) and **CIECAM02** (2002).
- The list of current-day uses: ANSI skin/hair color for forensic pathology, USGS soil color, prosthodontics tooth color, brewing beer color via Degrees Lovibond.

## Key claims

- **high** Munsell published *A Color Notation* in 1905; the modern *Munsell Book of Color* derives from the 1940s Optical Society of America renotation experiments.
- **high** Munsell was the first to separate hue, value, and chroma into perceptually uniform and independent dimensions.
- **high** The system is cylindrical but the achievable-color solid is *irregular* — different hues at the same value have different maximum chromas. Light yellow can reach chroma in the high 30s; light purple tops out much lower.
- **high** Notation: `H V/C` for chromatic colors, `N V/` for neutrals.
- **high** Munsell remains in use as a ground-truth reference for evaluating computer color-vision models.
- **medium** The Centore 2012 interpolation algorithm works better than the 2008 ASTM D 1535 method for converting non-book Munsell points.
