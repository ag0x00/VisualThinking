# Wikipedia — The Calling of Saint Matthew

## Summary

Reference article on Caravaggio's *The Calling of Saint Matthew* (1599–1600). Oil on canvas, 322 × 340 cm, housed in the Contarelli Chapel of San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome. The article covers commission history, subject matter, scholarly debate over which figure is Matthew, the painting's reception (including by Pope Francis), and a list of comparable treatments of the same Gospel scene by other artists.

## What it contributes

- Definitive provenance and commission history (commission dated July 1599; final payment July 1600).
- Scholarly debate notes: bearded-man-is-Matthew vs. slumped-young-man-is-Matthew, with citations to John L. Varriano's *Caravaggio: The Art of Realism* (2006) and Irving Lavin's "Caravaggio's Calling of St. Matthew: The Identity of the Protagonist" in *Past-Present* (UC Press, 1993).
- The "Last Adam" reading: Christ's hand mirrors Adam's in Michelangelo's *Creation of Adam* — explicit iconography from Romans 5:12–21 and 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45.

## Key claims

- **high** *The Calling of Saint Matthew* dates 1599–1600. Commission for the *Calling* + *Martyrdom* dated July 1599; final payment July 1600.
- **high** Housed in the Contarelli Chapel, San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome — remains in its commission location.
- **high** It was Caravaggio's first major church commission and his first painting with more than a handful of figures.
- **high** The painting features tenebrism: a stark contrast between light and dark (citing Smarthistory).
- **medium** Matthew's identity in the painting is contested; the bearded-figure reading is more common because the same model is unequivocally Matthew in the two companion paintings.
- **medium** Christ's pointing hand visually mirrors Adam's in the Sistine ceiling — read by some scholars as deliberate "Last Adam" iconography.

## Confidence notes

The Wikipedia article cites Smarthistory (Khan Academy partner), John Gash's *Caravaggio* (2003), Helen Langdon's *Caravaggio: A Life* (1998), and Irving Lavin's UC Press essay (1993). The factual scaffolding is well-sourced. Interpretation claims (Matthew's identity, the Adam comparison) are scholarly debate, not consensus — marked **medium**.
